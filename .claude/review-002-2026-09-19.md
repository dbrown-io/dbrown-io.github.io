# Review 002: safety, privacy, and what is still open

Date: 2026-09-19
Scope: the whole site, after the rebuild on `claude/teen-portfolio-review-1dyho6`
Mode: 1 (antislop applied during the work)

This is the part I did not change on my own. Everything below is a decision
for David and Rob, not a defect for me to fix.

## What the site currently says about a fifteen-year-old

Taken one at a time each of these is ordinary. The list is the point: together
they are a profile of a specific child, their routine, and when they are away
from home.

| On the site | Where | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Full name | Every page, title and JSON-LD | Ties everything else to a real, searchable identity |
| Face photo | Every page header | Makes him recognisable in the street |
| Age and year group | About, Experience | States plainly that the person being contacted is a minor |
| Service area, about five miles of Richmond | Services, JSON-LD | Narrows the search area considerably |
| Instagram and GitHub handles | Every page | Both link onward to more personal material |
| Rowing: cox, J16, "most days of the week" | Experience | A routine: where he is, and roughly when |
| Backstage crew, school theatre | About | Another fixed, findable location |
| Offers to attend a stranger's home alone | Services | Dog walking, pressure washing, garden work, PC repair |
| Offers boarding, so away from home overnight | Services | Signals when the house may be empty of him |

Nothing here is an emergency. It is worth one deliberate conversation rather
than staying the default it currently is.

### What I would change, in priority order

1. **Drop "most days of the week" from the rowing entry.** It is the only line
   that publishes a recurring schedule. The achievement reads just as well
   without it. This is the single highest value edit on the list and it costs
   nothing. Done: the entry now reads "a lot of the week" instead.
2. **Decide whether the age stays.** It currently appears twice. There is a
   real argument for keeping it: for the local audience it is honest, and
   hiding it would be worse when they meet him. For the professional audience
   it is context that makes the work more impressive, not less. My view is that
   it stays on About and Experience, and that this should be a decision rather
   than an accident.
3. **Reconsider the school theatre line** if the production or school is ever
   named alongside it. On its own it is fine.
4. **Do not publish a phone number.** This is why I left the WhatsApp option
   unwired. A WhatsApp link is a public phone number for a minor, permanently,
   and it is the one item on this list I would argue against outright. The
   booking form and an email address cover the same ground without it.
5. **Consider pointing formspree at a parent too.** The address is no longer
   published, so this is now a dashboard setting rather than a page change: a
   notification address that reaches Rob as well as David means an adult sees
   every enquiry before anyone turns up anywhere.

### Already handled

- `images/rowing.webp` is published, at David's decision. He confirms it is
  edited and that school logos are removed, leaving only the boat club's white
  and yellow. The privacy question is settled.

  The licensing question is not, and it is a separate one. The file still
  carries the photographer's watermark: an `allmarkone` account header across
  the top and `WWW.ALLMARK.ONE` burned across the bottom. A visible watermark
  is how a sports photographer marks an unpurchased proof. Using it on a page
  that advertises paid services is the kind of use they sell licences for, and
  cropping the watermark off would make it worse, not better. The fix is to
  buy that frame from allmark.one, or ask them for permission to use it with
  credit. Most regatta photographers will say yes to a credit link.
- The JSON-LD publishes only the locality, "Richmond, London, GB". No street
  address, no postcode, no phone number.
- EXIF and GPS: every image in the repo is clean. `profile.jpg` carried
  Photoshop metadata but no GPS, and it has been replaced by a stripped WebP.

## Open items, in the order they unblock things

### 1. Connect the booking form

Done. The endpoint is `https://formspree.io/f/xppwqqoq`, set as the form's
`action` so it also works with JS off.

Three settings still to check in the formspree dashboard: notifications going
to `dbrown.io@icloud.com`, reCAPTCHA off (it breaks AJAX submits), and the
allowed domain restricted to `dbrown-io.github.io`. Send one test submission
yourself, because formspree asks you to confirm the first one by email before
the endpoint is fully live.

The single "Email or phone" field named `reply_to` is gone. Formspree does not
just skip Reply-To without a field named `email`, it refuses the whole
submission with "email is missing", which is what broke the form on the first
live test. There are two fields now, a required `email` and an optional
`phone`, so submissions go through and replies are one click.

### 2. The email address

Set up, then deliberately taken back off the site on 2026-09-20. A `mailto:`
in the page source is exactly what address harvesters collect, and David did
not want his inbox on a public page. It is gone from the pages, the footers
and the JSON-LD.

Enquiries still reach him: formspree holds the notification address privately,
so the form works without the address being published. Instagram remains the
second route.

### 3. Prices

Settled: no published rates. David's reason is that price depends on timing
and several other factors, which is true of every job on the list, so a
headline rate would mislead more often than it helped. Every ticket says
"Quote first" and the Services page explains why. This is a decision now, not
a gap; do not fill it in later without David asking.

### 4. KidsHustle evidence

One video is linked from the KidsHustle entry. The "20K to 30K+ views per
video" claim stays out: one link does not evidence an average across all of
them, and the link is worth more than the number was.

Check the link opens for a logged-out visitor. I could not reach TikTok from
the sandbox to confirm it, and a dead evidence link is worse than none.

The App Store listing is still worth adding when there is one.

### 5. Year 10 mock grades

Done, stated plainly: 9s in Physics, Design Engineering and English Literature,
8s in Maths, Chemistry, Biology, French and English Language.

David supplied Biology as "8 (1 mark off a 9)". The parenthetical is out. It is
the same move as "several 8s and 9s across the board": it argues with the grade
instead of stating it, and a reader who sees five 8s and three 9s does not need
help being impressed.

### 6. Get one testimonial

I did not write a testimonials section, because antislop R-18 forbids inventing
one and an empty section is worse than none. One real quote from someone who
has booked him, with a first name and the job, would go above the form and do
more work than anything else on the page.

### 7. A custom domain

`dbrown-io.github.io` is a weak identity for someone whose pitch is that he
runs infrastructure. A domain also unblocks a proper mailbox. Once it exists,
every absolute URL in the JSON-LD, the canonical tags and `sitemap.xml` needs
updating.

### 8. Google Business Profile

Only David can create this, and it may require an adult. Without it the site
will not appear in the local pack or on Maps, which is where people actually
look for a dog walker. The JSON-LD is in place and ready to correspond to it.

## Things I deliberately did not do

- **No testimonials, no statistics, no invented prices.** R-17, R-18, R-36 and
  R-38, and they are the right rules here.
- **No parent named anywhere.** "A parent knows where I am" is on the Services
  page because David confirmed it. Nobody is identified.
- **No phone number.** See item 4 above.
- **The paper ground, dot grid, tape swatches, Caveat signature and the type
  set are untouched**, per the settled style direction in `AGENTS.md`.
